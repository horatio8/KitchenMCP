The gates

A checklist plus a signature

The signature is a Kitchen comment: attributed, timestamped, never edited. A deliverable cannot move forward through a gate without one. Diamond items ◇ are advisory; the rest are hard.

G1

Scope gate

Producerintake → scoped
  • A written brief exists and names the audience, the message and the one action it should drive
  • Deliverable type and workstream are set
  • A due date exists and is achievable
  • A named producer is assigned
  • Jurisdiction is recorded
  • Work is inside an accepted quote or retainer, or a quote has been raised
Why jurisdiction this early: it decides what text must appear in the artwork. Discovering it at G4 means a redesign.
G2

Production-ready gate

Producerscoped → in_production
  • All inputs on hand: copy direction, assets, data, brand profile
  • Any factual or negative claim has a cited, checkable source attached
  • Destination accounts and pages exist and are accessible
Start advertiser verification here. Meta political authorisation, Google election-ads verification and X verification all take days. They gate launch, not drafting. Starting them at G4 costs a week.
G3

Internal QA gate

Reviewerinternal_review → compliance_review
  • Output answers the brief — message, audience, action
  • Platform spec met: character counts counted, not estimated; aspect ratios; file types; durations
  • Brand profile respected: palette, type hierarchy, logo treatment
  • Copy proofread; names, dates, times, venues and URLs verified against source
  • Every link resolves and carries correct tracking parameters
  • The reviewer is not the producer
G4

Compliance gate

Compliancecompliance_review → client_reviewskipped for research · strategy-doc · internal
  • Authorisation, disclaimer or promoter statement present, correct and prominent for the jurisdiction
  • Paying entity confirmed and matching the platform-verified funding entity
  • Political advertising permitted on every destination platform
  • Advertiser verification complete on each platform
  • Special ad category declared where the platform requires it
  • Every negative or comparative claim carries an evidence reference
  • AI-generated or AI-altered media depicting real people, places or events is disclosed
  • Targeting is legal for the category and geographically fits the district
  • Flight dates clear any blackout, freeze or pre-poll restriction
  • A copy of the final creative is retained for the statutory record period
This gate runs automatically the moment a card enters the Compliance column.
G5

Client sign-off gate

Client approverclient_review → approvedskipped for internal
  • The approver is the person with authority to bind the client
  • Approval is explicit and in writing — never inferred from silence
  • The approval names the exact version being approved
  • Approval covers placement, spend and flight dates, not just creative
  • Any conditions attached to the approval are recorded and actioned
The most expensive mistake in the whole workflow is treating "looks good" in a group thread as an approval. It names no version, binds nobody and covers no spend. Record it properly or it did not happen.
G6

Deployment gate

Deployerapproved → deployed
  • What is being deployed is byte-identical to what was approved
  • Media is a real hosted file the platform can fetch — not a share link or preview page
  • Budget, bid strategy and schedule match the approved spend
  • Pixel and conversion tracking are live and firing
  • A named person can pause or pull this within minutes
The share-link trap: a Descript view URL is an HTML page, not a video. Facebook reports a problem with the file, Instagram throws a container error, YouTube rejects text/html. If a clip has no exported MP4, it has no post.
G7

Post-deployment verification

Deployerdeployed → verified
  • Asset confirmed live and rendering correctly on each destination
  • The authorisation statement is visible on the live placement
  • Delivery has begun — impressions or sends registering as expected
  • Live screenshot or permalink captured into the deliverable record
The gate everyone skips, and everyone regrets skipping.
Jurisdiction

What must appear in the artwork

Australia · authorisation statement
AUTHORISED BY {name},
{entity}, {town}
  • Required for paid electoral advertising, or anything communicated by or for a disclosure entity
  • Entity: name plus the town or city it is based in
  • Natural person on printed matter: name plus full street address
  • Paid creator content carrying electoral matter must also be authorised
  • State elections layer their own commission's rules on top
United States · disclaimer
PAID FOR BY {entity}
  • Must match the platform-verified funding entity — for client work it names the client's committee, not the agency
  • Meta ads live in the public Ad Library for seven years
  • Final-week freeze: new political ads are blocked in the last week; only ads with a prior impression keep serving. Pre-load closing creative
  • Google targeting capped at age, gender and postal code
New Zealand · promoter statement
PROMOTED BY {name},
{address}
  • Required at all times, not only during the regulated period
  • For paid social the statement goes in the ad itself
  • Must be clearly displayed — statements set too small draw complaints
  • Running without one risks a fine up to NZ$40,000
The trap

A federal authorisation is not a state authorisation

State elections run under their own Acts and their own commissions, and the address rules genuinely differ. The federal form is the one sitting in everybody's template, which is exactly why it is the one that fails.

RegionActPO BoxRequirement
SAElectoral Act 1985 s112ProhibitedName + street address. Party name or abbreviation for endorsed candidates. Small items exempt. Penalty to $5,000 / $10,000
NSWElectoral Act 2017ProhibitedName + street address. Must not look like an official NSWEC communication
VICElectoral Act 2002 s83AllowedStreet address or PO Box, never an email. Authoriser 18+. Obligation runs outside the election period too
QLDElectoral Act 1992AllowedResidential, business or PO Box; must be contactable there
WAElectoral Act 1907UnverifiedRequired once writs issue. Act amended Nov 2023 — confirm the current provision
TAS · ACT · NTOwn ActsUnverifiedConfirm with the commission
The engine checks the address form mechanically. A PO Box in an SA or NSW authorisation is a blocker. An email address in a Victorian one is a blocker. A town-only authorisation in SA — perfectly valid federally — is flagged as too thin. Where a region is marked unverified the engine says so rather than encoding a guess: a fabricated legal rule is worse than an admitted gap.

The metadata block

Kitchen has no custom fields, so structured data lives in a fenced block at the end of the description. Machine-written, machine-parsed, human-readable. The brief above it is yours; the block is the system's. Editing one never disturbs the other.

The evidence register

Every negative or comparative claim needs a source someone can check. The register lives in a second fenced block on the same task, so the claim and its backing travel together. The compliance check draws its claims from here, so the two can never disagree.

```evidence
id: E1
claim: Missed 22% of floor votes in the 2025 session
source: SA Hansard, 2025 session voting record, p.412
archived: https://web.archive.org/web/2026/hansard
status: verified
verifiedBy: James Flynn
--
id: E2
claim: Has taken donations from developers
status: unsourced          ← blocker
```
  • Never fabricate a source. If a claim cannot be backed, downgrade the ad or drop it — never launder it into a question. "Why won't he say whether…" is the same claim wearing a hat
  • Attaching a URL is not verification. Verified means a named person checked the source says what the claim says
  • Archive it. A live URL with no archived copy is flagged. The page that disappears is always the one you needed

Board columns

Existing column names are recognised automatically. Drafting, Doing and WIP all map to in_production; With Client maps to client_review. The SANDBOX board's five columns map with no renaming at all.

The audit trail

Four comment types, each machine-detectable by its marker: GATE-PASS, CLIENT-APPROVAL, COMPLIANCE-CHECK, STATE-CHANGE. Nothing edits or deletes a comment. The history is the record.

The record pack

What you produce when a regulator, a journalist or a client's lawyer asks what happened: what ran, who authorised it, who paid, who approved it and at which version, on what evidence, plus every gate signature, the compliance history and the state history. It also lists the gaps in its own record — because a record with gaps is still the record, and the time to fill them is while the people involved still remember.

The skill

All of it is packaged as a skill so it triggers on natural phrasing — "add this to the pipeline", "what's waiting on the client", "the client approved it", "pull the record for that ad" — rather than requiring anyone to remember nineteen tool names.

Enforcement, precisely

workflow_advance refuses when the transition is not legal in the state machine — and tells you what is; when the guarding gate has no recorded sign-off; and when the target is approved with no CLIENT-APPROVAL record.

force: true overrides — and writes "FORCED past G4 without a recorded sign-off" permanently into the audit trail. The escape hatch exists, and it leaves a mark.

Runbook

Standing up a client, and running one deliverable

New client

  1. Provision the board — thirteen columns
  2. Create milestones for election day and each flight window
  3. Add the client's approver as a Kitchen client user on the board
  4. Start advertiser verification on every platform in the plan, before any creative exists
  5. Record the authorisation statement and paying entity once; reuse on every deliverable

One deliverable

  1. Create it — brief, type, workstream, jurisdiction, due date
  2. Sign G1, advance to scoped
  3. Gather inputs and evidence; sign G2, advance to in_production
  4. Produce, then advance to internal_review
  5. The reviewer — not the producer — signs G3
  6. The compliance report posts itself. Clear the blockers, re-run, sign G4
  7. Client approves in writing, record the approval, sign G5
  8. Sign G6 and deploy
  9. Confirm live, capture the permalink, sign G7, archive

Weekly

  1. Anything in changes_requested beyond a week is a scoping failure — re-brief it
  2. Anything deployed but not verified is an unclosed loop
  3. Check verification status on every platform with a flight in the next fortnight

Scope and limits. This is operational guidance encoded as software, not legal advice. Jurisdiction-specific filing questions go to counsel.

The compliance engine is warn-only by mandate. It returns a verdict; a human decides what ships. LIKELY_REJECTED means "this will be taken down or rejected", not "the tool refused".

Policy state is mid-2026. Platform political-ad policy changes often — for policy-sensitive launches, re-verify against current published policy before relying on the matrix. The engine checks what it can see; it cannot tell you whether a claim is true, only whether a source is attached.
